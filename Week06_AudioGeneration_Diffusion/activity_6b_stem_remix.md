# Activity 6b: Stem Remix

**Week 6 — Thursday, in class (~30 minutes)**
**Format**: groups of 3–4

---

## Overview

Using stems pre-separated by HTDemucs, your group will create a 30-second remix using only the supplied audio — no new sounds. The constraint is the point: working within what already exists forces creative decisions about structure, balance, and narrative.

---

## The Stems

The instructor has provided four stems from a single track, separated using HTDemucs:

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `drums.wav` | Drum kit (kick, snare, hi-hats, cymbals) |
| `bass.wav` | Bass guitar or synthesizer bass |
| `other.wav` | Melodic instruments, pads, guitars, keys |
| `vocals.wav` | Lead and/or backing vocals |

All stems are the same length and in sync with each other. You may:

- Mute any stem for any duration
- Adjust the volume of any stem
- Time-shift a stem forward or backward (delay relative to original position)
- Loop a short section of a stem
- Reverse a stem or section (if your tool supports it)

You may **not** add new sounds, apply significant pitch-shifting, or use effects that are unavailable in the tools listed below.

---

## Tools

Use one of the following (based on what is available to your group):

- **GarageBand** (Mac) — import as separate tracks; use volume automation and mute buttons
- **Audacity** (cross-platform, free) — import as separate tracks; use Mute/Solo per track
- **BandLab in browser** (browser-based, no install) — [bandlab.com](https://www.bandlab.com)
- **Soundtrap in browser** — [soundtrap.com](https://www.soundtrap.com)

The instructor has the stems on a shared drive; download links are on the board.

## Debrief Questions

1. What constraints did you hit that you didn't expect?
2. Did the absence of the vocals change the character of the piece more or less than you expected?
3. What does stem manipulation feel like as a compositional act? Is it fundamentally different from composing from scratch?
4. Legally: if you released your remix, who would own it? Does the creative effort you put in change your answer?

---

## Instructor Notes

**Track selection**: choose a track that is cleared for educational use (e.g., a Creative Commons licensed track, or a track in the public domain). Avoid hit singles — the familiarity distracts from the compositional exercise. Good options: Freemusicarchive.org licensed tracks, Jamendo, or a track licensed under CC BY.

**Pre-separated stems**: run HTDemucs before class; confirm stems are clean (some tracks separate poorly — check for bleed). Provide stems as a shared Google Drive folder, USB drive, or course server.

**Timing**: 20 minutes for the remix work is tight. Encourage groups to spend 2–3 minutes planning (what do we want to do?) before touching the software. The groups that plan first produce better results.

**One-word response format**: writing words on the whiteboard creates a visual artifact that makes the debrief richer. Ask presenters to note discrepancies between intent and reception.
