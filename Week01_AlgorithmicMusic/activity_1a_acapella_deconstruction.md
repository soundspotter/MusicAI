# Activity 1a: Acapella Deconstruction

**Format:** In-class group activity
**Groups:** 4–5 students
**Time:** ~50 minutes (20 min preparation, 15 min performance, 15 min discussion)
**Focus:** Rock/Pop, 1980s–present

## Overview

Each group chooses a rock or pop song (1980s–present) and creates a live acapella arrangement — voice only, no instruments. The goal is to deeply understand the structural and functional layers of the music by reconstructing it from scratch.

## Task

### 1. Choose a song

Pick a song the whole group knows reasonably well. Good candidates have distinct parts that can be distributed across 4–5 voices. A few suggestions if you're stuck:

- "Africa" — Toto
- "Under Pressure" — Queen & David Bowie
- "Don't Stop Me Now" — Queen
- "Take On Me" — a-ha
- "Push It" — Salt-N-Pepa
- "Crazy in Love" — Beyoncé
- "Mr. Brightside" — The Killers
- "Rolling in the Deep" — Adele

### 2. Assign parts

Divide the song into **four functional layers**, with one person owning each. In a 5-person group, the extra person handles a second part or assists with arrangement:

| Layer | Responsibility |
|-------|---------------|
| **Melody** | Lead vocal line, including ornaments and phrasing |
| **Bass** | Bass line or low-frequency rhythmic content (bass guitar, kick drum fundamentals, 808 sub) |
| **Accompaniment** | Harmonic filler — chords, background vocal pads, guitar/keyboard stabs |
| **Percussion** | Rhythmic skeleton — snare, hi-hats, hand claps, mouth percussion, body percussion |

### 3. Map the song structure

Before you practice, sketch out the song form:

```
Intro → Verse → Pre-chorus → Chorus → Verse → Chorus → Bridge → Chorus → Outro
```

Note where each part enters, drops out, or changes character. Transitions between sections are your responsibility to arrange — decide collectively what changes to make at each seam (e.g., the bass simplifies before a chorus drop, percussion falls out for a bridge).

### 4. Practice and perform

Prepare a 1–2 minute excerpt that includes at least **two section transitions** (e.g., verse → chorus, or bridge → final chorus).

## Discussion Questions (after performances)

1. Which layer was hardest to abstract to voice? Why?
2. Where did your arrangement diverge from the original, and was that a choice or a constraint?
3. How did the transition moments force you to think about the song's structure differently?
4. What did this exercise reveal about the compositional decisions in the original recording?

## Connection to Algorithmic Composition

When you implement a song algorithmically (later in the course), you'll need to make exactly these same decompositions. Each layer you assigned here corresponds to an independent pattern in SuperCollider or Sonic Pi. The transitions you arranged by ear will become programmatic section changes. This exercise builds the musical intuition that makes algorithmic implementations sound intentional rather than mechanical.
